format_section:
  title: "How a review goes"
  steps:
    - title: "You bring one problem"
      text: "One job or one flow, not the whole platform. The nightly run that keeps growing, the retry that once did damage, the batch that stalls the database. Send a short description when you book, so we can prepare."
    - title: "We go through it together"
      text: "Thirty minutes on a call with Ronald Dehuysser or Nicholas D'hondt. Screen sharing welcome. We ask how it works today, where it hurts and what it has to do in a year. Classic solution architecture, no demo."
    - title: "You get it in writing"
      text: "Within a week you receive a one-page summary: the situation as we understood it, the issues we see, and our recommendation. Sometimes that is a JobRunr feature. Often it is a change in how the job is designed, and we say so."

what_we_ask:
  title: "What we ask"
  items:
    - "That you attended one of the trainings, live or on the recording"
    - "A short description of the problem when you book"
    - "Permission to use what we learn, anonymised, to improve JobRunr"

what_we_never:
  title: "What we never do"
  items:
    - "Turn the call into a sales pitch"
    - "Recommend JobRunr Pro when the fix is in your design"
    - "Share anything about your system with anyone"

who_section:
  title: "Who should take the review"
  text: "The developer who owns the jobs, together with whoever signs off on the architecture, if that is a different person. Reviews go best when both are on the call: one knows what the code does, the other knows what it has to guarantee."
